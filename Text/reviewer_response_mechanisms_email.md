Thank you for these comments. The rest of the committee pressed the same mechanism question at my last presentation, and pursuing it strengthened the thesis. I take your central question directly: how much of the climate–health-cost relationship cannot be explained by the agricultural income channel? To answer it, I measure agricultural dependence as a structural, pre-treatment county attribute and re-estimate each effect inside the least-agricultural counties, where any surviving effect cannot run through farm income. The identifying logic is that if agriculture were the whole story, the effect would vanish once the farm channel is removed. It does not.

Three results follow. To begin, the health-cost response carries no farm-income intermediary. One explanation for these relationships is that they operate through agricultural income; fortunately, county Medicare records (CMS Geographic Variation, 2014–2023) let me examine this directly. In fact, extreme heat raises standardized Medicare spending by roughly $112 per beneficiary and emergency-department visits by 8 to 10 per 1,000, a morbidity channel that would operate in any county with a hospital. Turning to income and employment, the effect there reflects broad labor exposure. The cold–employment loss survives and strengthens in the least-agricultural counties (−2,011 jobs, compared with −721 overall), and the heat–employment effect loads on a county's employment share in climate-exposed non-farm industries. This is the labor-productivity margin, and it is not what the agricultural story predicts.

The agricultural channel itself is real but narrow. It appears event-specific — driven by the 2012 Midwest drought cohort — and it partly reflects selective out-migration rather than same-population loss. Three further channels round out the picture. Energy burden — the share of household income spent on utility bills — is a distinct distributional margin, only weakly correlated with social vulnerability. On the supply side, the strain concentrates in safety-net hospitals. Migration I report as a caveat on the drought scar rather than a clean channel.

I have also made your two definitional points explicit in the technical note. Every shock is defined relative to the county's own long-run distribution, and multi-hazard exposure is absorbed through lags, a compound indicator, and a cumulative count of shock-years.

Taken together, these results indicate that agriculture is one channel among several: the climate–health-cost relationship runs primarily through directly measured morbidity, broad labor exposure, and energy burden. Full detail is in the mechanism analysis (`Analysis/mechanism/`) and technical-note §§1.2, 1.3, 6. I would welcome your read on whether this bounds the agricultural channel to your satisfaction.
